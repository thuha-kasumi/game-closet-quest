CLOSET QUEST · VERSION 1.1 · PHOTO CLOSET BETA

A real piece of your closet.
A new possibility with friends.

This package upgrades your existing, working Render game. You do not need another repository, another Render service, or any API key.

New now: a private browser photo closet; name, category, vibe and location labels; guided illustrated shapes and colors; solo and multiplayer use of personal pieces; backup export/import; a clearly labeled roadmap; and one easier file for editing your main wording.

1. Upload the update to your existing repository

  1. Download Closet-Quest-Final-Update.zip and extract it.
  2. Keep the earlier starter ZIP as a backup.
  3. Open your existing game-closet-quest repository on GitHub.
  4. Choose Add file → Upload files.
  5. Drag all files inside the extracted folder into the upload area. Put them at the repository root, where your current package.json is. Do not upload the ZIP itself or an enclosing folder.
  6. GitHub will update matching filenames and add the new files. Commit them together with the message Add photo closet beta and editable site copy.
  7. Watch your existing Render service deploy. If automatic deployment is off, use its Manual Deploy → Deploy latest commit action.
  8. When it is live, reload your existing game URL. If you still see the old navigation, use Ctrl+Shift+R. The new navigation contains Play together · My Closet · What’s next · How to play.

Upload the whole set together. The updated interface imports new modules, and the server must also accept custom illustrated garments. Updating only the visual files would leave the personal-clothing feature incomplete.

A redeploy resets old game rooms. Create a fresh room for testing. My Closet data is stored separately in each browser under the same game URL and ordinarily survives a server redeploy. Clearing browser data, switching browser/device/URL, or ending a private session can make that local closet unavailable.

2. Try the new feature before submitting

  1. Open My Closet in a normal browser window. Select a JPG, PNG or WebP clothing photo from your device. HEIC is not supported; export it to JPG first if needed.
  2. Give it a name, choose its category, choose the closest illustrated shape, and adjust the suggested color. The suggestion samples the photo and may need correcting, especially if the background dominates.
  3. Add Where is it? such as “Blue suitcase” or “Top shelf,” and tags such as “warm, autumn, campus.”
  4. Choose Add to My Closet. You should see the photo thumbnail beside its illustrated stand-in and location.
  5. Refresh. Confirm the saved piece remains in that browser. Try searching for its location or tags.
  6. With no room joined, click Try in solo styling. The illustrated piece should appear on the character. Check another category to combine it with starter clothes.
  7. Return to Play together, create a new room, and join as a second player in a private window or on another device.
  8. Start styling. Choose My pieces to select your saved garment. The second player’s My Closet should be empty unless they add their own items.
  9. Lock both looks. Check that the other player sees your chosen illustrated shape/color and the piece’s name at reveal. They should not receive the photo, tags or storage location.
  10. Vote, reach results and complete a round. Try Save outfit card to check the custom color appears in the downloaded image.
  11. Return to My Closet and use Export closet backup. It downloads a private JSON file with the photo thumbnails and labels. Import a backup merges saved items by their identity.
What this beta actually does: it compresses your clothing photo into a small thumbnail on your device, then lets you choose a standard illustrated shape and color. It does not automatically trace the garment, remove its background, reproduce its pattern, recognize its category, or simulate realistic fit.

This beta holds up to 24 saved pieces per browser. Storage availability varies; if saving fails, the app shows an error rather than claiming success. Export a backup to preserve your collection outside that browser. A backup contains personal photos and location notes, so keep it private.

3. Edit your wording yourself

Most main wording is now in content.mjs. On GitHub, open that file and click its pencil/Edit button. Change the words between double quotation marks, keep the commas and keys, then commit. Render redeploys your update.

Find this key in content.mjsWhat it changes
brand, taglineBrand text in the navigation; tagline on the home-page illustration sticker.
heroEyebrowSmall text above the home-page headline
heroLine1, heroLine2The two large home-page headline lines
heroDescriptionThe paragraph below that headline
homeNoteThe paragraph explaining the real-life closet problem
homeNotePlacement"home" shows it on the home page; "help" moves the paragraph into How to play; "hidden" hides it
homeNotePositionWhen shown at home, "above" puts it above the three-step strip; "below" puts it below
footerLeft, footerRightThe two footer messages
closetTitle, closetIntro, closetPrivacyMy Closet heading and introductory explanations

visionNow, visionNext, visionLaterThe three roadmap descriptions, separated by availability

Example: move the story into How to play

homeNote: "My idea started with my roommates’ forgotten clothes.",
homeNotePlacement: "help",

Use \n for a new line inside the supported paragraphs. For example:

heroDescription: "Rediscover what you own.\nStyle it with your people.",

Keep straight double quotes around a value. An apostrophe such as roommates’ is fine. To put an actual double quote inside a sentence, write \", or avoid double quotes in the sentence.

4. Add a showcase photo or video later

The What’s next page works without any media. It contains an optional media panel that appears only when configured.

  1. Prepare your own image or video and name it showcase.png, showcase.jpg, showcase.webp, showcase.mp4, or showcase.webm.
  2. Upload it to the repository root. A short MP4 with standard browser-compatible encoding, comfortably below GitHub’s 25 MB browser-upload limit, is the simplest video choice.
  3. In content.mjs, find showcase. Change the empty path to your filename with a leading slash, and edit its caption. Example:
showcase: {
  path: "/showcase.mp4",
  caption: "Concept demonstration. Automatic tracing shown here is a future idea."
}

Image/video display is chosen from the file extension. To hide the panel again, set path: "". Caption any mockups or future-only demonstrations clearly. This beta’s server serves only the listed showcase filenames.

5. Updated submission description

Keep the existing cover and live home-page URL. Review this text and use it after confirming that the update works on your deployed game:

Closet Quest turns a real dorm-room problem—forgetting what clothes we own and how to combine them—into a multiplayer styling party. Two to six players join from their own devices, dress cartoon avatars for a shared occasion, reveal their looks together, and rate occasion fit and creativity across three rounds. The new My Closet beta connects the game to a player’s real wardrobe. Players upload a clothing photo, add its category, vibe tags and storage location, then choose a matching illustrated shape and color. The original photo stays beside the illustrated style card for reference. Players can browse and search saved pieces, try them in solo styling, or wear their illustrated versions during a multiplayer round. Photos and location notes remain in the player’s browser; only the illustrated garment details are shared in the game. Closet backups can be exported and imported. Illustration is guided by the player, rather than automatic tracing or realistic virtual try-on. I developed the idea and built this version with ChatGPT. Future possibilities are clearly labeled in the What’s next page. No player account or installation is required. Rooms reset on server restart, and the free demo host may take about a minute to wake after inactivity.
Before submitting: publish the update, test a real photo in a normal browser, confirm a second player sees the illustrated version at reveal, check scoring, and submit the live game URL. The earlier conflict between the mission-page and official-rules deadlines remains unresolved.

Validation during preparation: 19 automated game/storage/server checks passed. A separate test ran isolated client modules through the photo-input flow with a canvas shim and completed a multiplayer game with custom illustrations; no photo data appeared in the game API requests.
